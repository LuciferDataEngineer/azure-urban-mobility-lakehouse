## Trip data

NYC Taxi & Limousine Commission publishes the [official trip record catalogue](https://www.nyc.gov/site/tlc/about/tlc-trip-record-data.page), including the exact CloudFront download links used here. The input scope is **historical yellow-taxi trips for January–March 2024**, selected to match the project blueprint. It is not a live mobility feed. The publisher warns that provider-supplied records may be inaccurate and that schemas can change. Those limitations motivate immutable raw retention and explicit contracts.

The [2024 NYC Open Data entry](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/Transportation/2024-Yellow-Taxi-Trip-Data/rwwi-khbc) and [Data.gov catalogue](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/2024-yellow-taxi-trip-data) provide additional discovery metadata. Downloaded file digests, byte counts, retrieval times and source schemas are retained in local manifests. They identify the bytes observed now; they do not prove what was originally published in 2024. The large trip files and row-level derived data are excluded from Git. Only aggregate results and the small public zone lookup are committed.

The zone lookup is a **November 2026 reference snapshot**, not a version historically verified for 2024. `reference/manifest.json` records its SHA-256 and source. It has one row for each LocationID 1–265, including special/unknown zones. A lookup join never changes the trip count. Zone 264/265 must not be interpreted as precise geography.

## Definitions

The [TLC yellow trip dictionary](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/tlc/downloads/pdf/data_dictionary_trip_records_yellow.pdf) describes recorded meter timestamps, miles, pickup/dropoff zone IDs, and monetary amounts. `total_amount` excludes cash tips. Summing recorded charges does not measure operator profit, take-home income or all cash revenue. The current dictionary includes a congestion fee introduced in 2025; the selected 2024 inputs predate it. Additional fields are retained in raw data and catalogued without silently changing existing metrics.

## Why this topic is relevant now

The MTA's [16 October 2026 capital-program update](https://www.mta.info/agency/bridges-and-tunnels/congestion-relief-zone/better-transit) describes ongoing transport investments supported by congestion tolling. Reliable historical mobility data supports descriptive planning questions about demand by place and hour. This repository does **not** estimate the impact of congestion pricing: its three 2024 months precede the program and are insufficient for causal inference. Extending the contract to 2025–2026, adding comparison periods and an identification strategy would be separate work.
